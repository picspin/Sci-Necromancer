# Blind review checklist

## Ethics and consent

- Identify human, animal, prospective, interventional, retrospective, registry, public, or synthetic data use.
- Check whether ethics approval, exemption, consent/waiver, trial registration, and conflicts/funding are stated when applicable.
- Treat missing information as unknown, not approved.

## De-identification and privacy

- Look for names, initials, exact dates, record numbers, institution-specific identifiers, faces, burned-in annotations, rare combinations, or other re-identification risks.
- Flag unsupported claims that data were anonymized or compliant.

## Data integrity

- Compare every quantitative claim with the supplied source.
- Check denominators, subgroup totals, sample flow, units, dates, confidence intervals, p-values, effect estimates, and internal arithmetic.
- Flag implausible precision, impossible timelines, circular validation, leakage, or conclusions stronger than results.

## Methods and reporting

- Check design, setting, eligibility, reference standard, outcomes, missing data, validation, statistical analysis, uncertainty, and limitations.
- Apply STARD for diagnostic accuracy; TRIPOD/TRIPOD+AI for prediction models and AI-enabled prediction; and conference-specific rules where relevant.
- Do not assert compliance from keywords alone.

## Citation integrity

- Extract DOI, PMID, and sufficiently specific author-year or title candidates.
- Compare authors, title, venue, year, identifiers, and whether the cited work supports the claim.
- Distinguish a real citation from a correctly interpreted citation.

## Conference compliance

- Check the declared conference and submission type, required structure, limits, blinded-content rules, disclosures, and prohibited promotional language.
- Mark time-sensitive rules as provisional unless current official guidance was supplied or verified.
